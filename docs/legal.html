Legal and privacy

The short version: this is a hobby page for a group of friends. It shows their public Steam stats, it has no ads, no tracking and no cookies, and anyone can ask to be removed. The detail is below.

Who runs this

This page is run by Adam, a private individual in Dundee, Scotland, as a non-commercial hobby project. It makes no money and sells nothing.

To ask a question, ask for something to be removed or raise a complaint, open an issue on the project's GitHub page. If you know Adam, you can also just tell him.

Privacy notice

Adam is the person who decides what happens to the personal data described here (the "controller" under UK data protection law).

What's shown about the players

  • The Steam ID, Steam display name, avatar and profile link of each player on the board.
  • Their Rust stats and hours played, which Steam makes available when a player's profile and game details are set to public.
  • A nickname the group uses for them.

All of this is pulled from Steam every six hours and published here, which means anyone on the internet can see it. It is also stored in the project's public GitHub repository, including earlier versions of the data.

Why, and on what basis

Each player on the board is a friend who asked to be on it. That agreement is the legal basis (consent), and they can take it back whenever they like. Players with private Steam profiles show up with no stats.

What visitors should know

  • There are no cookies, no analytics, no adverts and nothing stored in your browser.
  • The fonts are served from this site, not from Google.
  • The page is hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when you load it, as any host does, and handles them under its own privacy statement.
  • Your browser also asks GitHub for the list of screenshots, and loads player avatars from Steam's servers. Those services see your IP address too. Adam gets none of this.
  • Clicking a player's name takes you to their Steam profile, which has its own rules.

If you upload a screenshot

Uploads need the squad password. The image, its caption and the password go through a small Cloudflare Worker, which passes the image on to GitHub, where it becomes public. The file name includes the upload date and your caption. No account or name is attached to the upload.

How long it's kept

Stats are overwritten every six hours, but the older versions stay in the repository's history. Screenshots stay until they are taken down.

Your rights

If you're on the board or in a screenshot, you can ask to see what's held about you, have it corrected, have it removed or object to it being used. Ask through the GitHub page and it will be sorted without any fuss. Removing someone from the board and the current data is quick. Clearing old versions out of the repository's history takes a bit longer, and it will be done too if you ask.

If you think your data has been mishandled, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk. You're welcome to raise it with Adam first.

Screenshots and takedowns

Screenshots are shared by the squad. By uploading one you're saying that:

  • you took it, or you have permission to share it;
  • anyone identifiable in it is fine with it being public;
  • it doesn't contain personal details such as real names, addresses or private messages, and nothing abusive, hateful or illegal.

Game footage and screenshots of Rust include Facepunch's artwork. They're shared here as fan content.

If something on the page is yours, or shows you, and you want it gone, open an issue on the GitHub page with a link to it. It will be taken down promptly, and you don't need to explain why. Copyright owners can say so in the same message.
